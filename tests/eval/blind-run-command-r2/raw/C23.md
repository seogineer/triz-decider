## Application ideas
### Principle #2 Taking Out (Extraction)
- **Idea 1 (pull the disturbing part out of the object):** Move the bulky battery out of the watch body. Put the extra capacity in a strap-embedded battery or a clip-on band module. The case stays thin while the total capacity grows.
- **Idea 2 (keep only what is truly needed):** Keep only a small, always-needed core in the case, such as the timekeeping and sensing circuits. Move the high-drain functions (GPS, cellular, always-on display) into a phone or a detachable module.

### Principle #19 Periodic Action
- **Idea 1 (replace continuous with pulsed):** Turn continuous heart-rate and SpO2 measurement into short sampling bursts. Also drive the display with a low-refresh pulsed scheme. This extends runtime without adding cells.
- **Idea 2 (change the frequency):** Change the sampling frequency with context. Sample rarely when the wearer is at rest or asleep, and more often during exercise.
- **Idea 3 (use the pauses):** Use the idle time between pulses for deep sleep on the main processor. Run background sync in batches during those windows.

### Principle #30 Flexible Shells and Thin Films
- **Idea 1 (thin films in place of bulky structures):** Use a thin-film or flexible battery that follows the curve of the case back or strap. This uses the empty volume under a curved case that a rigid cell cannot fill.
- **Idea 2:** Replace the thick metal battery can with a thin pouch or film encapsulation to gain capacity per millimeter of thickness.

### Principle #10 Preliminary Action
- **Idea 1 (carry out the change before it is required):** Pre-charge from ambient energy. A solar film on the dial and thermoelectric harvesting from body heat top up the battery before it is needed.
- **Idea 2 (position objects ahead of time):** Pre-compute and cache data, for example workout summaries, so the heavy processing is not done at the moment of use.

### Principle #9 Preliminary Anti-Action
- **Idea 1 (cancel a harmful action in advance):** Battery swelling and heat are the harmful effects of packing a large cell into a thin case. Pre-compensate by leaving an expansion gap in the design, or by limiting the charge ceiling in advance. This protects a thin, dense cell over the years.

## Next steps
- **Best combination:** #19 (lower average power) and #30 (more capacity per thickness) attack both sides of the trade-off. #2 offers a structural alternative if the thin case is a hard requirement.
- **Numbers to check:**
