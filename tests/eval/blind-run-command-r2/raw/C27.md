## Contradiction
Adding redundant valves to the pump raises its reliability, but it makes the pump harder to build and to service.

## Application ideas
### Principle #1 Segmentation
Sub-principles used: "Build it so it is easy to assemble and take apart" and "Split the object into parts that can work independently".
- Put each valve in a cartridge module. It has its own seat and seals and connects with quick-connect ports. A failed valve is swapped in minutes without opening the pump.
- Make the redundant valves independent, so each one works on its own. Then one valve can be isolated and serviced while the pump keeps running.
- Design the valves as a standard bank of identical cartridges, like a battery pack of cells. This cuts the number of unique parts and simplifies assembly.

### Principle #11 Beforehand Cushioning
Sub-principle used: "Prepare protective measures beforehand to make up for an object's low reliability".
- Keep the redundant valve as a passive standby that only takes over on failure, like a reserve parachute. It needs no extra control logic, so complexity stays low.
- Build the pump to tolerate a valve failure, and schedule service by condition. Repairs are then planned rather than done in an emergency.

### Principle #13 The Other Way Round (Inversion)
Sub-principles used: "Do the opposite of the action the problem calls for" and "Fix the part that used to move and move the part that used to be fixed".
- Do the opposite of adding valves: use one robust valve and make the seat the thing that moves or is swapped. Wear then lands on a cheap seat insert instead of a whole valve.
- Turn the assembly around, so the valve cartridge is inserted from the outside of the housing. Service no longer means dismantling the pump body.

### Principle #35 Parameter Changes
Sub-principle used: "Change its degree of flexibility".
- Replace some rigid valve parts with flexible ones, such as an elastomer duckbill or diaphragm valve. These have fewer parts and are simpler to assemble.
- Let one flexible element do the work of a whole redundant valve stack. This lowers the part count while keeping the fault tolerance.
