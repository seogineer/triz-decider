## Contradiction
Raising the conveyor's speed makes its rollers wear out sooner and last a shorter time.

## Applied ideas
### #28 Mechanics Substitution
- **Non-contact support:** Use magnetic or air-cushion support for the load, so less of it rides on the roller surface (sub-principle: use electric, magnetic or electromagnetic fields). This suits light, flat items.
- **Drive by field:** Use linear-motor or magnetic drive zones. The rollers then carry the load and no longer transmit traction, so slip and friction wear drop at high speed (sub-principle: turn a fixed field into a moving one).

### #33 Homogeneity
- **Matched contact materials:** Make the roller surface and the belt or load-contact surface from the same or a very similar material, such as polyurethane on polyurethane. This reduces abrasive and adhesive wear between them.

### #1 Segmentation
- **Modular roller sleeves:** Make the roller from separate parts, such as a replaceable outer sleeve on a fixed core (sub-principle: easy to assemble and take apart). At high speed only the sleeve wears and it is quick to swap.
- **Independent zones:** Split a long roller line into independently driven segments. Each segment then carries less load and slip, and you can find the worn one.

### #18 Mechanical Vibration
- **Reduce vibration-driven wear:** At high speed, out-of-round rollers and resonance accelerate wear. Check that the operating speed is away from the resonant frequency, and balance the rollers (sub-principle: exploit the resonant frequency, used here to avoid it).
- **Vibration to shed debris:** Add gentle ultrasonic or piezoelectric vibration to shed buildup that acts as an abrasive.

### #3 Local Quality
- **Zoned surface:** Use a hard, wear-resistant coating or hardened material only where load and slip are concentrated, such as the ends or the loading and transfer zones (sub-principle: replace a uniform structure with one that differs by region).
- **Tune by position:** Match the diameter or surface hardness to local speed and load, since the accelerating and transfer zones wear the most.

## Next steps
- **Find where the wear is:** Measure wear by roller position, and whether it comes from slip, impact or contact. The best principle depends on that: #28 or #3 for slip, #33 for material pairing, #1 for maintenance cost.
- **Re-check the mapping:** If bearing or shaft wear is the real issue, re-map the worsening parameter (for example #27 reliability) and look it up again.
- **Trial:** Test one or two candidates, such as a zoned coating (#3) and a replaceable sleeve (#1), on a single line. Compare roller life at the target speed.
