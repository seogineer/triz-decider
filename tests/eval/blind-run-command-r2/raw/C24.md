## Contradiction definition
Making the bridge cables thinner to reduce the amount of material makes them vibrate more, and reliability, strength and service life get worse. Thinner cables fail earlier.

## Application ideas
### Principle #28 Mechanics Substitution
- **Use a field instead of a mechanical means:** Fit magnetorheological or electromagnetic actuators or dampers on the cables. Their damping is then applied through a field, not by adding steel.
- **Turn a constant field into a changing one:** Drive the damper with a controller that reads accelerometers and adjusts damping to the wind and rain conditions at the time. This is semi-active damping.

### Principle #10 Preliminary Action
- **Do the change beforehand:** Pre-stress the cables to a target tension. This raises the natural frequency and lowers fatigue stress amplitude before any vibration starts.
- **Position objects ahead of time:** Install damper brackets and cross-ties during construction, so they act from the start.

### Principle #3 Local Quality
- **Make regions differ:** Put dampers, heavier sheathing or thicker wall only in the part of each cable near the anchorage. That is where bending fatigue concentrates, so the rest can stay thin.
- **Match each region to its role:** Give the outer sheath a helical rib or dimpled surface to break up rain-wind excitation, and leave the load-bearing core smooth.

### Principle #2 Taking Out (Extraction)
- **Extract the disturbing property:** Move the vibration-prone behaviour out of the cable. Link neighbouring cables with cross-ties so a long, flexible span becomes several short, stiffer ones.
- **Keep only what is needed:** Use a tuned mass damper that removes just the problem frequency band.

### Principle #27 Cheap Short-Living
- **Replace one expensive object with several cheap ones:** Use several thin parallel strands instead of one thick cable, each with a fast inspection or replacement cycle. This applies only if a strand can be replaced and the bridge design allows redundant load paths.

## Next steps
- **Check the mapping:** Confirm whether the cable was the main load path (strength, #14) or a stay-cable failure driven by fatigue and rain-wind vibration (#16, #27). If failures are mostly fatigue, #16 is the more accurate worsening parameter.
- **Measure first:** Record cable natural frequencies and damping ratios, and check whether the vibration comes from wind, rain-wind interaction or traffic.
- **Test the best candidates:** Simulate or field-test the combination of pre-tension (#10), dampers or cross-ties (#2, #28) and surface shaping (#3). Compare fatigue life and total material use against the original thick cable.
- **Involve a structural engineer:** These are conceptual ideas. Any design change to a real bridge needs qualified structural and safety review.
