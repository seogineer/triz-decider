## Contradiction
Making the bridge cables thinner reduces the amount of material and the dead weight of the structure. As a result, the cables vibrate, and their strength, service life and reliability get worse. The contradiction statement was confirmed in your request, so I did not ask again.

## Application ideas
### #28 Mechanics Substitution
- Replace passive stiffness with active control (field-based sub-principle). Use magnetorheological or electromagnetic dampers, or an active tuned-mass system driven by acceleration sensors. This suppresses vibration without adding steel.
- Turn a constant into a changing field (sub-principle 3). Adjust damper force or stay-cable tension in real time to match wind and rain conditions, instead of over-sizing the cable for the worst case.

### #10 Preliminary Action
- Carry out the change before it is needed (sub-principle 1). Pre-tension the cables, and fit helical strakes or a dimpled surface to the cable sheath in the factory. These break up wind-induced vortex and rain-wind excitation before it builds up.
- Position dampers ahead of time (sub-principle 2). Install viscous dampers near the anchorage at construction so they act instantly.

### #3 Local Quality
- Replace a uniform structure with one that differs by region (sub-principle 1). Keep the cable thin along most of its length. Thicken, or add composite sleeves and reinforcement, only at the anchorages and clamps, where fatigue is worst.
- Give each region a different function (sub-principle 2). A smooth mid-span can carry load, and a textured mid-span can spoil aerodynamics. Use different cross-ties or damper spacing on the longest cables.

### #2 Taking Out
- Pull the disturbing property out of the object (sub-principle 1). Separate the vibration-prone part from the load-carrying part. Add cross-ties or a secondary damping cable network that removes the vibration mode without thickening the main cable.

### #27 Cheap Short-Living
- Replace one expensive object with several cheap ones (sub-principle 1). Replace one thick cable with several thin, cheaper strands or parallel cables. Each strand is a low-cost, easily replaced unit, and the strands can be damped against each other.
- Treat the outer aerodynamic sheath as a cheap, replaceable layer. It is worn by weather instead of the structural strand.
