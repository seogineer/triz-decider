## Application ideas

### Principle #2 Taking Out (Extraction)
- **Idea 1 (pull the disturbing part out of the object):** The battery is the bulky part, so move it out of the case. It could go into a battery band or strap, or into a clip-on back module. The case then keeps only the display, sensors and chip.
- **Idea 2 (keep only what is truly needed):** Move power-hungry, rarely used functions such as GPS, LTE and heavy compute into a phone or a detachable pod. The watch itself then needs less battery.

### Principle #19 Periodic Action
- **Idea 1 (replace continuous action with pulses):** Sample heart rate and other sensors in short bursts instead of continuously. Duty-cycle the radio, and refresh the display in pulses.
- **Idea 2 (change the frequency):** Adjust the sampling rate to activity, for example slow at rest and fast during workouts.
- **Idea 3 (use the pauses):** Use the idle gaps between pulses for background sync and for charging from harvested energy. That means solar or body heat, with the radio kept asleep.

### Principle #29 Pneumatics and Hydraulics
- **Idea 1 (gas or liquid in place of solid parts):** Replace solid battery-related parts with a fluid. One option is a flow or liquid-electrolyte cell. Another is a thin, flexible pouch cell with gel electrolyte that fills the odd voids inside the case.

### Principle #9 Preliminary Anti-Action
- **Idea 1 (counter the harm in advance):** Anticipate the thickness that the battery will add. Design the case in advance so the battery sits in space that is already there, such as the strap lugs or the curved back. The battery then adds no extra thickness.

### Principle #10 Preliminary Action
- **Idea 1 (position ahead of time):** Place the battery ahead of time in the case's dead zones, for example a ring-shaped cell around the display module. It then sits where it is most convenient.
- **Idea 2 (do the change before it is required):** Pre-compute or cache data and pre-render watch faces, so the watch spends less energy at run time.
