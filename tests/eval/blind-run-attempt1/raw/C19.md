## Application ideas

### #35 Parameter Changes
- **Flexibility:** Use roller sleeves in a softer, more elastic material such as polyurethane. This absorbs impact at high speed instead of wearing through. It can also be stiffer at the core and softer at the surface.
- **Temperature:** Higher speed raises friction heat. Cool the roller surface or bearings with airflow, or choose a compound whose properties hold at the higher operating temperature.

### #28 Mechanics Substitution
- **Fields instead of contact:** Replace the mechanical contact that causes wear. Use magnetic or air-cushion support in the sections that run fastest, so the load is not carried by rubbing surfaces.
- **Field replaces mechanical drive:** Use linear-motor or magnetic drive on the fastest sections. The rollers then only guide, and no longer transmit drive force.

### #10 Preliminary Action
- **Pre-positioned wear layer:** Fit rollers with a replaceable sleeve or a pre-hardened coating. Swap sleeves on schedule before wear damages the roller body.
- **Pre-conditioning:** Bring the load up to belt speed before it meets the roller, for example with a slow-to-fast accelerating zone. This avoids slip and scuffing at contact.

### #18 Mechanical Vibration
- **Reduce vibration:** Speed excites resonance, and the vibration adds to wear. Find the roller's resonant frequency and set the speed or roller pitch to stay off it.
- **Piezo damping:** Add piezoelectric or active damping at the bearing mounts in place of passive mechanical fixes.

### #3 Local Quality
- **Zoned roller design:** Use a different roller construction in each region. The impact-loading zone gets a hard-wearing surface, and the steady-run zone gets a lighter, low-friction one.
- **Speed zones:** Run only the sections that need the throughput at high speed, and keep sections with heavy loading or transfer points slower.

## Next steps

- **Confirm the wear mode.** The right principle depends on the cause: abrasion, slip, heat, or resonance. Measure roller surface temperature, slip rate, and vibration at the new speed.
- **Check the mapping.** I chose #15 and #23 for wear. If the actual failure is bearing failure, #27 Reliability may be a better worsening parameter, and re-running the lookup would be worthwhile.
- **Trial the cheapest ideas first.** A softer or replaceable sleeve (#35, #10) and a speed-zone or resonance check (#3, #18) need the least capital. Try them before magnetic drive (#28).
