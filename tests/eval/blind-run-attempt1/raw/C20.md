## Application ideas

### Principle #35 Parameter Changes (sub-principle: change its degree of flexibility)
- Idea 1: Use a wing skin and internal structure whose stiffness varies with conditions. It stays flexible at low load, so it can change camber for cruise and takeoff. Under high load or high speed, it stiffens through a variable-stiffness spar or a shape-memory alloy or piezoelectric layer. Strength then does not depend on a rigid, fixed shape.
- Idea 2: Lock the wing's stiffness with temperature or an electrical signal. Shape changes happen while the wing is soft, and a rigid state is locked in before high-G maneuvers.

### Principle #10 Preliminary Action (sub-principle: carry out the needed change partly before it is required)
- Idea 1: Preset the wing's configuration on the ground or before a flight phase, for example before takeoff or before descent. Then the mechanism doesn't have to move under peak aerodynamic load.
- Idea 2: Put reinforcement and actuators in place ahead of time along the load path. That way each adaptation happens from a position already prepared to carry the load.

### Principle #40 Composite Materials (sub-principle: replace a single-material object with a composite)
- Idea 1: Build the wing from composites with tailored fibre direction, so it bends and twists in a controlled way for adaptation but stays strong along the main load direction.
- Idea 2: Use a composite skin over the morphing sections. It carries load without needing the hinges and gaps of a conventional flap.

### Principle #3 Local Quality (sub-principle: let each region take a different function)
- Idea 1: Make only the trailing edge and the outer wing morphing and flexible. Keep the wing box, root and main spar as rigid, uniform load carriers.
- Idea 2: Use different materials and stiffness by region: flexible where shape change is needed, and dense or reinforced where bending loads are highest.
