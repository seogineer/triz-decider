## Applying the principles

### #35 Parameter Changes
- **Flexibility (sub-principle 3):** Give the roller a softer or more elastic outer layer, such as polyurethane, or a resilient sleeve. This absorbs impact and slip at high speed and cuts abrasive wear.
- **Temperature (sub-principle 4):** Faster running raises friction heat, which softens the surface and speeds wear. Add cooling airflow or use heat-resistant materials so the surface stays within its stable temperature range.

### #28 Mechanics Substitution
- **Fields instead of mechanical contact (sub-principle 2):** Use magnetic or air-cushion support for the parts of the line where rollers mainly carry load. This removes contact and friction there.
- **Moving field (sub-principle 3):** Move the load with linear-motor or magnetic drive on the fast sections. The rollers then only guide the load instead of carrying the full drive force.

### #10 Preliminary Action
- **Do the change beforehand (sub-principle 1):** Apply a wear-resistant coating or surface hardening to the rollers before installation. Alternatively, run a break-in cycle first.
- **Position in advance (sub-principle 2):** Keep pre-assembled spare roller cassettes at the line. Worn rollers can then be swapped in one step without stopping the line for long.

### #18 Mechanical Vibration
- **Reduce or control vibration:** Vibration rises with speed and adds fatigue wear. Check the roller's resonant frequency (sub-principle 3) and keep the operating speed away from it. Adjusting the roller spacing or drive frequency is one way to do this.
- **Piezoelectric option (sub-principle 4):** Use a piezoelectric element as a vibration or wear sensor. It can flag early degradation, which also feeds the maintenance planning under #10.

### #3 Local Quality
- **Different regions, different functions (sub-principles 1 and 2):** Wear is not uniform. Reinforce only the high-wear zones with hardened or replaceable rings. Examples are the load-bearing centre, the accelerating and decelerating sections, and the transfer points. The rest of the roller can stay cheap.
- **Suit each region to its conditions (sub-principle 3):** Use tougher rollers in the fast sections and standard rollers in the slow ones.
