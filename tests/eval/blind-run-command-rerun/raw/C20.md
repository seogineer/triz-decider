## Contradiction
Making a wing adaptable to different flight conditions (a morphing wing) makes its structure weaker.

## Parameter mapping
| Role | Parameter | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Improve | #35 Adaptability or versatility | The wing has to suit different speeds, loads and flight phases. |
| Improve | #12 Shape | The adaptability comes from changing the camber, span or sweep of the wing. |
| Worsen | #14 Strength | "Structurally weaker" means less ability to carry loads without breaking. |
| Worsen | #13 Stability of composition | Joints and hinges in a moving structure deform, fatigue and wear. |

## Application ideas
### Principle #35 Parameter Changes
- **Change its degree of flexibility:** Use a wing box whose stiffness changes with the mode. It stays stiff in high-load cruise and manoeuvres and softens only when the wing needs to reshape. Options include shape-memory alloy or variable-stiffness composite spars, or pressurised or lockable internal cells.
- **Change its physical state or temperature:** Use low-melting-point or jammable core elements. They soften briefly during a shape change and re-stiffen when locked. The wing is only flexible while it is actually morphing.

### Principle #30 Flexible Shells and Thin Films
- **Use flexible shells in place of bulky structures:** Make the morphing trailing edge or leading edge a flexible skin over a compliant substructure. This replaces hinged flaps and slats, which add gaps, joints and stress concentrations. The rigid main spar keeps carrying the primary load.
- **Isolate the object from its environment with a flexible membrane:** An elastomeric skin seals the moving interior mechanism from airflow. This limits the wear and deformation of the mechanism and the aerodynamic loading on it.

### Principle #14 Spheroidality (Curvature)
- **Replace flat surfaces with curves:** Use curved, arch-like ribs and corrugated or curved skin panels in the morphing region. Loads then spread along the arch instead of concentrating at a hinge. This is the arch-bridge idea from the principle's examples.
- **Use round elements such as rollers and spirals:** Use rolling or rotary joints instead of sliding hinges. Load transfer is smoother and wear is lower.

### Principle #3 Local Quality
- **Make regions differ in function:** Keep the root and main box rigid and strong. Make only the outer trailing-edge or tip zones compliant and adaptive. Adaptability and strength are then assigned to different regions rather than traded off across the whole wing.

### Principle #32 Color Changes
- **Make a hard-to-see object visible:** This is the weakest fit for the problem. It applies to condition monitoring rather than to the contradiction itself. Use strain-sensitive coatings or embedded fluorescent or luminescent markers in the flexible zones. They show fatigue or damage early, so a lower safety margin can be tolerated.
