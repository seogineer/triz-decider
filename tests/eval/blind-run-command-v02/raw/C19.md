Raising the conveyor's **speed** worsens the rollers' **wear/durability** — they wear out much sooner.

## Applied Ideas

### #3 Local Quality
- **Zone the roller surface, don't build it uniform.** Use a harder, more wear-resistant coating or material (e.g., hardened steel, ceramic-coated, or tungsten-carbide-sleeved) only at the high-load/high-slip contact zones of each roller, while keeping the rest lighter/cheaper — matches "let each region take a different function."
- **Vary roller spacing or diameter along the conveyor**, giving zones that carry heavier or faster-moving loads larger-diameter or more heavily built rollers, so no single roller design has to survive worst-case conditions everywhere.

### #19 Periodic Action
- **Replace continuous rotation/full-speed operation with pulsed or intermittent drive** on unloaded or return-path rollers — they don't need to spin continuously at the belt's full speed if nothing is on them, cutting wear cycles.
- **Rotate/index individual rollers periodically** (e.g., advance each roller a few degrees on a schedule) so wear is spread around the roller's circumference instead of concentrating on the same contact line.

### #35 Parameter Changes
- **Change the roller surface's degree of flexibility or hardness as a function of speed** — e.g., a roller with a resilient elastomer layer over a rigid core absorbs shock and reduces surface friction/wear at higher belt speeds compared to a uniformly rigid roller.
- **Actively adjust lubricant viscosity or bearing preload** with speed (thinner lubricant / lower preload at high speed) to cut frictional wear as speed increases.

### #5 Merging
- **Combine multiple narrow rollers into a single wider, ganged roller assembly** sharing load across more bearing surface, reducing the wear concentration any one roller experiences at higher throughput.
- **Merge the roller and bearing/lubrication system into one sealed cartridge unit** so wear-critical components (bearing + roller surface) are serviced/replaced together, keeping the fast-wearing part isolated and easily swappable.

### #10 Preliminary Action
- **Pre-treat or pre-harden roller surfaces (case hardening, pre-lubrication, break-in run) before installation**, so rollers enter high-speed service already conditioned to resist the higher wear rate, instead of wearing in under production load.
- **Pre-position spare/replacement rollers at each station** so that when a roller does wear out faster (an accepted trade-off of speed), swap-out is instant and doesn't cost line downtime — shifting the real problem from "prevent wear" to "make wear cheap to handle."
