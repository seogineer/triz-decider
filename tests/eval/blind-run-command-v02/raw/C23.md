## Contradiction Definition
Increasing the smartwatch's **battery duration** (to last a week) worsens the **case thickness/volume**.

## Applied Ideas

### #2 Taking Out (Extraction)
- Move part of the battery capacity out of the main case: an energy-storing strap/band (battery-in-strap), so the case itself stays thin while total wearable energy grows.
- Extract non-essential, power-hungry components (e.g., always-on GPS chip) onto a companion module or offload to the paired phone, so the case only needs to power what's truly required — reducing the capacity (and thickness) the case-mounted cell must provide.

### #19 Periodic Action
- Replace continuous sensor polling (heart rate, SpO2, GPS) with duty-cycled/burst sampling, so a week's worth of runtime is reached with a smaller cell instead of a physically bigger one.
- Pulse the display and radio (BLE) wake-ups instead of keeping them always active, stretching battery life without adding capacity/thickness.

### #29 Pneumatics and Hydraulics
- Use a flexible/pouch-type (soft-pack) lithium-polymer cell shaped to the curved underside of the case instead of a rigid flat/cylindrical cell, filling the case's curved internal volume more efficiently and packing more capacity into the same thickness.

### #9 Preliminary Anti-Action
- Add a small buffer capacitor that pre-charges to absorb peak current draws (radio bursts, display flashes), letting the main cell be optimized purely for energy density rather than peak power delivery — enabling a thinner, higher-capacity-density cell.

### #10 Preliminary Action
- Pre-form a custom-shaped (stepped/L-shaped) battery during design so it fills otherwise-unused internal volume around the display driver, lugs, and antenna — increasing effective capacity without adding case height.

## Next Steps
