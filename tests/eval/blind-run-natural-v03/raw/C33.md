## Application ideas
The housing and the mechanism it protects are not described, so these ideas are generic.

### Principle #35 Parameter Changes
- **Degree of flexibility:** make the housing stiff only where the impact load falls, such as corners and mounting points. Keep the areas the user grips or presses (buttons, covers, latches) at their original, more flexible design. This should keep the added stiffness from adding operating effort.
- **Physical state:** use a stiffness-variable material or a filled or foamed structure. It absorbs impact but stays easy to move or open. This is my reading of the "variable stiffness" example.

### Principle #32 Color Changes
- **Marking:** use contrasting colour or luminescent marks on the controls, latches and openings that the reinforcement now makes bulkier or less obvious. Users can then find and operate them without hunting.
- **Transparency:** make a window or transparent section over the parts users must see, so the thick housing does not hide them.

### Principle #30 Flexible Shells and Thin Films
- **Thin films:** replace thick reinforcing plates with a thin, tough film or laminate skin. It keeps strength up while cutting the bulk and weight that make the device awkward to handle.
- **Flexible membrane:** put a flexible membrane over the buttons and controls. The rigid housing protects the device, and the controls stay easy to press.

### Principle #2 Taking Out (Extraction)
- **Pull the disturbing part out:** move the reinforcement off the parts users touch and put it in a separate frame or bumper. The operating parts stay light and the frame takes the load.
- **Keep only what is needed:** check which reinforcement is truly required. Remove it from areas that carry no load.

### Principle #22 Blessing in Disguise
- **Use the harmful factor:** use the added mass or stiffness of the housing as a stable base or grip, for example a weighted body that stays put while a control is pressed.
